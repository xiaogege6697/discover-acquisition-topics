# Research Playbook

Use this reference when collecting current demand and competition signals.

## Source priority

Prefer sources in this order when available:

1. First-party transactions, CRM, customer interviews, sales and support conversations.
2. Direct customer behavior: search queries, comments, reviews, complaints, returns, and community questions.
3. Competitor offers, landing pages, advertisements, content, public pricing, and unanswered comments.
4. Official platform guidance, regulators, laws, standards, industry associations, and primary research.
5. Reputable secondary reporting and analysis.

Do not treat a creator's opinion, a single viral post, or search-result count as market proof.

## Minimum evidence map

For a practical lightweight study, seek:

- 20–50 pieces of customer language across at least two source types;
- 5–10 relevant competitors or substitute solutions;
- 3–5 repeated triggers, pains/desires, objections, and attempted alternatives;
- evidence of an existing transaction, budget, time cost, or meaningful consequence;
- at least one source on current context for regulated or rapidly changing markets.

Use smaller samples when access is limited, but lower confidence explicitly.

## Queries to explore

Search with customer language rather than only industry terminology:

- “怎么办 / 为什么 / 能不能 / 值不值得 / 后悔 / 避坑 / 被坑 / 不给 / 失败”
- “适合谁 / 怎么选 / 对比 / 多少钱 / 流程 / 时间 / 真实体验”
- role + trigger: “离职后提成”, “自由职业尾款”, “第一次装修增项”
- alternative + dissatisfaction: “用了X还是…”, “找了X没解决…”
- consequence: “不处理会怎样”, “错过时间”, “证据没了”

Adapt language to the market and platform.

## Signal interpretation

Classify each observation:

- **Behavioral:** purchase, booking, repeated use, switching, cancellation, complaint.
- **Verbal:** question, stated need, objection, aspiration.
- **Competitive:** many providers, repeated promises, bid/advertising intensity.
- **Contextual:** regulation, technology, seasonality, demographic or cultural change.

Behavioral evidence normally outweighs verbal enthusiasm. Repeated customer language outweighs the researcher's preferred terminology.

## Human interaction protocol

When interviews or conversations are available, ask:

1. What happened, in the customer's words?
2. Why did it matter at that moment?
3. What did they try first?
4. What made the existing option unsatisfactory?
5. What outcome did they want, and what did they fear?
6. What evidence or information did they lack?
7. What made them act, delay, buy, or walk away?
8. How would they describe the issue to a friend or type it into search?

Do not lead the interviewee toward the desired product. Ask about past behavior before hypothetical willingness.

## Competition audit

Record for each competitor:

- target customer;
- dominant topic and promise;
- proof and credibility signals;
- format and platform;
- offer, price signal, and CTA;
- engagement quality rather than raw engagement;
- repeated unanswered questions;
- obvious sameness or trust weaknesses.

Whitespace is valuable only if customer evidence exists and the business can deliver.

## Research integrity

- Date all volatile information.
- Prefer primary sources for legal, medical, financial, policy, and platform claims.
- Separate fact, inference, and hypothesis.
- Do not infer market size from a few posts.
- Do not invent keyword volume, competitor revenue, or conversion performance.
- Respect privacy; do not expose identifiable customer information without authorization.
