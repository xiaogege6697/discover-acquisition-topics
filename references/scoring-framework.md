# Topic Scoring Framework

Score each candidate from 0 to the maximum shown. Use evidence, not intuition alone.

| Dimension | Max | High-score standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Customer specificity | 8 | A distinct person recognizes themselves immediately |
| Trigger clarity | 8 | A concrete event or decision makes the topic timely |
| Pain/desire intensity | 12 | Meaningful money, time, risk, identity, emotion, or aspiration |
| Demand evidence | 14 | Repeated current behavioral or verbal evidence from multiple sources |
| Decision gap | 10 | Free information does not fully resolve the contextual judgment |
| Willingness and ability to pay | 10 | Expected value plausibly exceeds price and effort |
| Competition whitespace | 10 | Demand exists and current content/offers serve it poorly or generically |
| Service and delivery fit | 12 | The business can credibly fulfill a useful next step |
| Proof and trust potential | 6 | Cases, process, demonstrations, data, credentials, or artifacts are available |
| Channel and format fit | 5 | The intended audience and behavior match the platform format |
| Interaction potential | 5 | The topic naturally elicits useful questions or qualification signals |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Risk adjustment

After the base score, reduce or disqualify:

- minus 5–15: mostly attracts spectators, peers, or freebie seekers;
- minus 5–15: requires excessive education before the customer feels value;
- minus 5–20: weak economics, low collection probability, or costly delivery;
- minus 5–20: high reputation, privacy, or platform-policy risk;
- disqualify: fabricated facts, deceptive scarcity, guaranteed outcomes, unlawful solicitation, unqualified professional advice, or manufactured fear/conflict;
- disqualify: no credible service path or delivery owner.

For regulated industries, a material compliance risk can override the numerical score.

## Interpretation

- **80–100:** priority test, provided evidence quality is adequate.
- **65–79:** promising; tighten audience, trigger, proof, or offer.
- **50–64:** supporting or awareness topic, not a primary acquisition bet.
- **Below 50:** deprioritize unless it serves a deliberate portfolio role.

Scores compare candidates within the same market study; they are not universal market forecasts.

## Portfolio selection

Do not select only the highest-scoring version of one idea. Build a portfolio with:

- one problem/desire recognition topic;
- one trigger-moment topic;
- one decision/comparison topic;
- one proof or case topic;
- one trust-building “when not to buy/act” topic.

Prefer three distinctive hypotheses over ten cosmetic title variations.

## Topic brief fields

For each finalist record:

- working title;
- specific audience;
- trigger moment;
- customer verbatim;
- pain/desire and consequence;
- misconception or decision gap;
- free useful action;
- proof needed;
- interaction prompt;
- qualification questions;
- next offer;
- platform and format;
- evidence strength;
- score and risk adjustment.
