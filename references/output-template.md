# Output Template

Adapt the length to the user's request. Lead with the strongest conclusion.

## 1. Recommendation

State:

- the customer segment to prioritize;
- the trigger or unmet decision to own;
- the buying engine;
- why this area is attractive now;
- what not to pursue yet.

## 2. Scope and assumptions

List geography, audience, offer, price/trust level, channels, research dates, and important unknowns.

## 3. Evidence map

Summarize:

- repeated customer language;
- strongest pain/desire and triggers;
- attempted alternatives and objections;
- evidence of payment, consequences, or action;
- competition patterns and whitespace;
- confidence and contradictory evidence.

Cite public sources close to current external claims.

## 4. Ranked shortlist

Use a compact table:

| Rank | Topic/title | Audience + trigger | Why it can acquire | Service bridge | Score | Risk |
|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|---|

Include 8–12 topics for a full study or 3–5 for a lightweight request.

## 5. Top briefs

For the best 3 topics provide:

- customer verbatim and situation;
- hook/title;
- content promise;
- 4–6 point outline;
- proof needed;
- interaction question;
- qualification path;
- appropriate CTA and next offer;
- platform-native adaptations;
- compliance/reputation notes.

## 6. Test plan

Define a 1–2 week minimum test:

- number and mix of pieces;
- what hypothesis each tests;
- one controlled variable per variation;
- owner and response process;
- success and stop criteria.

Prefer business metrics:

- qualified interaction rate;
- qualified inquiry count;
- screening completion;
- booking/demo/visit rate;
- paid conversion;
- gross profit or expected customer value;
- irrelevant lead rate;
- complaints and policy violations.

## 7. Learning loop

End with what to collect from every interaction and how it will update the next topic cycle. Make clear that the ranking is a current hypothesis until real customer behavior validates it.
